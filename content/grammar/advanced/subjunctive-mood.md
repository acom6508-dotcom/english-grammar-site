## What is the Subjunctive Mood?

The subjunctive mood is a verb form used to express wishes, suggestions, demands, or hypothetical situations. While less common in modern English than in other languages, it remains important in formal writing and certain fixed expressions.

## Forms of the Subjunctive

### Present Subjunctive

The present subjunctive uses the base form of the verb for all persons (no 's' for third person singular).

**Structure:** Base form of verb (be, go, do, etc.)

**Common uses:**

1. **After verbs of suggestion, recommendation, or demand:**
   - I **suggest** that he **study** harder.
   - The doctor **recommends** that she **take** vitamins.
   - We **demand** that the company **compensate** the workers.

2. **After adjectives expressing importance or urgency:**
   - It is **essential** that everyone **be** present.
   - It is **crucial** that the report **be submitted** on time.
   - It is **important** that you **arrive** early.

### Past Subjunctive

The past subjunctive uses "were" for all persons when expressing hypothetical or contrary-to-fact situations.

**Examples:**
- If I **were** rich, I would travel the world.
- I wish I **were** taller.
- She acts as if she **were** the boss.

## Common Constructions

### 1. That-clauses After Certain Verbs

Verbs that trigger the subjunctive:
- **suggest, recommend, propose**
- **demand, request, require**
- **insist, urge**
- **ask, command, order**

**Examples:**
- The teacher **suggests** that John **study** more.
- The board **recommends** that the policy **be** changed.
- I **insist** that she **come** with us.

### 2. That-clauses After Adjectives

Adjectives that trigger the subjunctive:
- **essential, vital, crucial, critical**
- **important, necessary**
- **imperative, urgent**
- **advisable, desirable**

**Examples:**
- It is **essential** that he **understand** the risks.
- It is **necessary** that all students **register** by Friday.
- It is **imperative** that we **act** now.

### 3. Wishes and Hypothetical Situations

**Current wishes (contrary to present reality):**
- I wish I **were** fluent in Spanish. (I'm not fluent)
- I wish she **knew** the answer. (She doesn't know)

**Past wishes (contrary to past reality):**
- I wish I **had studied** harder. (I didn't study hard)
- He wishes he **had been** more careful. (He wasn't careful)

### 4. If I were you... Construction

This common expression always uses "were":
- If I **were** you, I would accept the offer.
- If I **were** in your position, I'd reconsider.

## Subjunctive vs. Indicative

### With "that" clauses:

**Subjunctive (suggestion/requirement):**
- I suggest that he **go** to the doctor. (base form)

**Indicative (statement of fact):**
- I know that he **goes** to the doctor. (normal present tense)

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Adding 's' to third person

❌ Incorrect: I suggest that he goes to bed early.
✓ Correct: I suggest that he **go** to bed early.

### Mistake 2: Using 'was' instead of 'were'

❌ Less formal: If I was president...
✓ Formal: If I **were** president...

### Mistake 3: Forgetting the subjunctive after certain verbs

❌ Incorrect: It is essential that she is here on time.
✓ Correct: It is essential that she **be** here on time.

## Fixed Expressions

Some common phrases always use the subjunctive:
- **God save the Queen!**
- **Long live the king!**
- **Be that as it may...**
- **Come what may...**
- **So be it.**
- **Far be it from me...**

## Practice Exercises

Choose the correct form:

1. The committee recommends that the proposal (is/be) accepted.
2. If I (was/were) you, I would apply for the scholarship.
3. It is crucial that everyone (understands/understand) the instructions.
4. I wish I (was/were) better at mathematics.
5. The law requires that all citizens (vote/votes) in elections.

## When to Use the Subjunctive

### Formal Writing
The subjunctive is more common in:
- Academic writing
- Legal documents
- Formal business communication
- Official recommendations

### Informal Speech
In casual conversation, the subjunctive is often replaced with the indicative:
- Formal: I suggest that he **take** a break.
- Informal: I suggest he **should take** a break.

## Summary

The subjunctive mood is used to express:
- Suggestions and recommendations
- Requirements and demands
- Wishes and hypothetical situations
- Urgency and importance

While it may seem complex, mastering the subjunctive will elevate your English to a more sophisticated level, especially in formal contexts.
